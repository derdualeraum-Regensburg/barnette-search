## Verified bibliography

The bibliography contains fourteen peer-reviewed entries plus the plantri 5.8
manual: Katona (1966), Dickson (1969), Spencer (1970), Cai (1984), Honkala,
Karpovsky, and Litsyn (2003), Falgas-Ravry et al. (2014), Balogh et al. (2016),
Letzter (2024), Bonamy et al. (2023), Botler and Naia (2025), Holton, Manvel,
and McKay (1985), Brinkmann and McKay (2007), Alt et al. (2016), and Gorsky,
Steiner, and Wiederrecht (2023). All fifteen entries are cited; no duplicate
keys remain.

## Positioning retained in the manuscript

| Literature branch | Relationship to hsep |
|---|---|
| Separating systems | Ordinary separation requires distinct incidence signatures. Katona and Dickson provide classical finite-set context. |
| Complete separation and antichains | Complete separation covers both orientations of every pair, so signatures are incomparable. Dickson and Spencer provide classical context; the manuscript retains a direct specialization proof. |
| Sperner theory | The binomial counting obstruction is the standard antichain bound and is not claimed as new. |
| Path separation | Falgas-Ravry et al., Balogh et al., Bonamy et al., and Letzter use paths under the same or a closely related directed separation condition. |
| Cycles, subdivisions, and identification | Botler and Naia allow a broader test family than Hamiltonian cycles of one fixed graph; Honkala, Karpovsky, and Litsyn represent cycle-based identification. |
| Graph-specific separation | Cai is cited only as broad context; the manuscript does not claim that Cai's definition is identical to hsep. |
| Cubic Hamiltonicity and perfect matchings | Holton, Manvel, and McKay give Barnette-class Hamiltonicity context. The complement/bit-reversal observation is derived without claiming prior study. |
| Barnette's conjecture and generation | Alt et al. and Gorsky, Steiner, and Wiederrecht provide conjectural and matching-theoretic context; Brinkmann and McKay and the plantri manual support generation. |

The wording remains deliberately cautious: the set-system notions are
classical, while the apparent distinction is the requirement that every test
be a Hamiltonian cycle of the fixed graph. The manuscript says only, “To the
best of our knowledge,” that this restricted strong-separation parameter has
not previously been investigated.

## Editorial decision on Test Cover terminology

The earlier draft named Test Cover/Minimum Test Collection without a direct
verified citation. The focused revision removes those names and the associated
contrast. This avoids an unsupported literature detour without affecting the
mathematics or the broader separating-systems positioning.
