\section{Tied orders and open extremal conjectures}
\label{sec:conjectures}

\subsection{Tied maximizers in the certified range}

The extremal behavior at orders congruent to two modulo four is structurally
different from the uniform double-ladder recurrence.

\begin{computationalresult}[Certified tied-order structure]
\label{res:tied-structure}
Every one of the 17 tied maximizers at orders \(22,26,30,34\) contains a
3-cycle component in the face-adjacency graph induced by quadrilateral faces,
as well as at least one strict ladder component.  The full quadrilateral
component signatures are pairwise distinct within each order and reproduce
the tie counts \(2,5,3,7\).

Twenty-five exact square-gadget certificates link these graphs to earlier
examples.  Thirteen links are literal ladder-cell insertions.  In the other
twelve, the same four-vertex gadget is inserted on two suitable disjoint edges
of a 6-, 8-, or 10-face end-cap region.  Thus these graphs are not asserted to
be members of one \(D(a,b)\) recurrence.
\end{computationalresult}

\subsection{Open extremal continuation}

For each order divisible by four through 36, the unique certified maximizer is
the balanced or nearly balanced double ladder listed in Computational
Result~\ref{res:double-ladder-recurrence}.  The exact value of \(\hsep(D(a,b))\) is
now known for all odd \(a,b\ge3\), but this does not compare \(D(a,b)\) with
all other Barnette graphs at the same order.

\begin{conjecture}[Extremal continuation]
\label{conj:extremal-continuation}
At orders divisible by four beyond the certified census range, the balanced
or nearly balanced double ladder is the unique Barnette graph maximizing
Hamiltonian edge separation.
\end{conjecture}

Proving Conjecture~\ref{conj:extremal-continuation} requires order-level arguments or
complete competing-graph certificates; the exact identity
\eqref{eq:exact-double-ladder} alone is insufficient.  In particular, this
manuscript does not claim \(\MB(40)=60\).  The previously recorded linear
fit to the three tied-order values at \(26,30,34\), which predicts
\(\MB(38)=39\), remains an especially weak extrapolation rather than a
conjecture supported by a transfer proof.  No order-38 computation is reported
here.
