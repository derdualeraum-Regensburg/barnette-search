\section{Computational method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Census generation and graph validation}

Complete non-isomorphic censuses were generated with plantri 5.8
\citep{plantri58}.  For target order \(n\), the dual generation command has the
form
\begin{center}
\texttt{plantri -b -c3 -d T -}, \qquad \(T=n/2+2\).
\end{center}
The binary \texttt{planar\_code} stream retains the rotation system.  Each
record was checked for the requested order and edge count, simplicity,
cubicity, bipartiteness, planarity, 3-connectivity, and Euler and face
arithmetic.  Canonical hashes were computed from the sphere embedding; graph6,
planar code, canonical edge lists, rotations, and faces were retained in the
certificates.  Generation indices were required to be contiguous and canonical
hashes unique.  Census completeness was never inferred from a retained
candidate subset.

The plantri command, version, executable digest, environment, worker count, and
runtime were recorded per order.  At most four workers were used.  Expensive
stages used deterministic ordering, atomic checkpoints, and resumable output.

\subsection{Hamiltonian cycles and separating covers}

For a cubic graph of order \(n\), there are \(m=3n/2\) edges and therefore
\(m(m-1)\) ordered edge-pair requirements.  At order 36 this is
\(54\cdot53=2,862\) requirements per graph.  Every emitted cycle was checked
for closure, vertex uniqueness, spanning, and edge validity before it was used
as a set-cover column.

For the smaller censuses, the complete undirected Hamiltonian-cycle universe
was enumerated for every graph and the set-cover problem was solved exactly.
For larger censuses, the pipeline first constructed deterministic separating
covers for every graph and ranked the difficult tail.  Once a candidate value
\(k\) had an exact lower and upper certificate, a competitor needed only a
verified cover of size at most \(k\), or at most \(k-1\) to establish uniqueness.
Complete-universe enumeration and exact optimization were then restricted to
graphs that could still attain the maximum.  A timeout or incomplete universe
was not accepted as a conclusion.

\subsection{Three levels of evidence}

The proof pipeline deliberately separates the following objects.
\begin{description}
  \item[Solver output.] A proposed cycle, cover, optimum, or infeasibility
    result produced by a search or optimization implementation.
  \item[Emitted certificate.] Canonically encoded graphs and explicit cycles,
    together with either packing requirements or a certified complete cycle
    universe suitable for exhaustive exclusion.
  \item[Independent verification.] A standalone Python standard-library
    program reparses the artifacts, checks graph and cycle structure, evaluates
    every ordered requirement, checks lower certificates, order summaries, and
    SHA-256 manifests, and does not import the project, NetworkX, plantri, SAT,
    or optimization packages.
\end{description}

For every nonmaximizer the final package contains an independently verified
cover strictly smaller than the maximum at that order.  For every maximizer it
contains an exact primal certificate and either a matching packing or a
complete-universe exhaustive lower proof.  Thus a greedy witness count is never
reported as \(\hsep(G)\).
