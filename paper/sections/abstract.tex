We study the Hamiltonian strong edge-separation number \(\hsep(G)\).
Abstractly, it is the minimum size of a completely separating subfamily of the
Hamiltonian-cycle hypergraph of \(G\): for every ordered pair of distinct edges
\((e,f)\), some selected cycle contains \(e\) and avoids \(f\).  An exhaustive
certificate audit covers all 22,263 non-isomorphic Barnette graphs through
order 36.  Exact upper and lower certificates establish every maximizer, while
verified strict upper certificates exclude all competitors.
At orders divisible by four in this range, the unique maximizers
form a double-ladder family related by a four-vertex square insertion.  We
define this family, classify its Hamiltonian cycles, and prove for all odd
\(a,b\ge3\) that
\[
 \hsep(D(a,b))=\frac{(a+2)(b+2)-1}{2}.
\]
Matching symbolic constructions prove the two bounds: a packing of ordered
edge requirements gives the lower bound, while a boundary-plus-parity family
of Hamiltonian cycles gives the upper bound through an antichain of incidence
signatures.  Independently verified, prediction-locked computations confirm
three larger family members.  These checks do not establish order-level
extremality beyond the census range; whether balanced double ladders remain
globally extremal after order 36 is open.
