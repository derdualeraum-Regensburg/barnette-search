\section{The certified finite extremal sequence}
\label{sec:sequence}

\begin{computationalresult}[Certified values through order 36]
\label{res:sequence}
The complete Plantri 5.8 censuses and independent certificate verification give
the values in \cref{tab:extremal-sequence}.  Across the nonempty orders, 22,263
non-isomorphic graphs were audited.  Every maximum and tie count was
independently verified, and every nonmaximizer has a verified separating cover
strictly below the maximum of its order.
\end{computationalresult}

Every cubic graph has even order, since the degree-sum identity gives
\(3|V|=2|E|\).  Hence all odd orders from 9 through 35 are impossible.  The
complete order-10 Plantri census is empty.  These undefined entries are not
optimization failures.

The order-36 statement is especially useful as a regression target:
\[
 \MB(36)=49,
\]
with a unique maximizer of canonical hash
\begin{center}
\small\nolinkurl{2f96ada16c46cd2bd038b97d5af44f46ed14522cba1edf3fa041d66e02107bcc}.
\end{center}
Its complete universe contains 68 undirected Hamiltonian cycles.  A 49-cycle
primal certificate and a 49-requirement packing certificate prove its exact
value.  All 15,373 competitors have independently checked covers of size at
most 48.

Matching packings certify the displayed maximizers at orders
\(8,12,16,20,24,28,32,36\).  At orders \(14,18,22,26,30,34\), at least one
displayed exact result uses exhaustive set-cover exclusion over a certified
complete cycle universe instead.  This distinction concerns the form of the
lower certificate, not the exactness of the result.

\begin{statusnote}
\Cref{res:sequence} is an exhaustive finite computational theorem about the
listed orders.  It is not a proof of any formula for arbitrary \(n\), nor does
it assert anything about orders 38 or 40.
\end{statusnote}
