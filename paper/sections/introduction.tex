\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Separating systems encode elements by incidences with selected tests.
Ordinary separation requires distinct codes, while complete separation
requires incomparable codes.  In this paper the ground set is the edge set of
a graph and every admissible test is a Hamiltonian cycle.  This restriction
couples a classical set-system condition to global spanning structure.

We investigate the resulting parameter on Barnette graphs: simple, planar,
bipartite, cubic, 3-connected graphs.  Four levels of evidence are kept
distinct:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
  \item ordinary mathematical propositions proved in the text;
  \item finite computational results supported by explicit, independently
        checkable certificates;
  \item prospective computations whose values were frozen before evaluation;
        and
  \item open extremal conjectures beyond the certified census range.
\end{enumerate}

The computation audits all 22,263 non-isomorphic Barnette graphs through order
36 and determines every extremal value in the nonempty censuses.  Exact upper
certificates are separating families; lower certificates are packings or
exhaustive exclusions over certified complete Hamiltonian-cycle universes.
No greedy cover size is reported as exact.  Table~\ref{tab:extremal-sequence}
records the sequence.  At orders divisible by four, the unique maximizers form
the double-ladder family shown in Figure~\ref{fig:double-ladder-family}; the
tied orders congruent to two modulo four have a different face structure.

The finite pattern leads to an infinite-family theorem.  A rail/rung
recurrence classifies all Hamiltonian cycles of \(D(a,b)\) for odd
\(a,b\ge3\).  A symbolic packing supplies a lower bound for \(\hsep\), while a
boundary-plus-parity family of Hamiltonian cycles supplies a matching upper
bound through an incidence-signature antichain.  Consequently,
\[
 \hsep(D(a,b))=\frac{(a+2)(b+2)-1}{2}
 \qquad(a,b\ge3\text{ odd}).
\]
This determines the invariant on the infinite family, but not its extremality
among all Barnette graphs of the same order.  Prediction-locked checks confirm
three larger instances; global extremality of the balanced family beyond order
36 remains open.
